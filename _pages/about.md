I am an economist with training in **Economics and Economic Policy** and an interest in using applied economic research to understand development challenges and inform evidence-based policy.

My research interests centre on **international economics, international finance and health economics**. I am particularly interested in how exchange-rate movements, international financial conditions and pharmaceutical trade affect medicine affordability, healthcare access and household welfare in developing economies.

My previous research and professional experience also includes agricultural and environmental economics, climate adaptation, gender and household welfare, and the evaluation of social and health programmes.

## Research interests

- **International economics and trade:** pharmaceutical trade, import dependence and trade exposure.
- **International finance and macroeconomics:** exchange-rate pass-through, external shocks and monetary institutions.
- **Health economics:** medicine affordability, healthcare utilisation and health financing.
- **Development and applied microeconomics:** household welfare, gender and policy evaluation.
- **Agricultural and environmental economics:** climate adaptation and agricultural resilience.

## Current research

My emerging research agenda examines the links between international financial shocks, exchange-rate movements and access to essential medicines. A proposed comparative African–Asian research programme will investigate pharmaceutical import dependence, medicine affordability and supply-chain resilience, subject to a careful assessment of data availability.

I am also working on research concerning central bank independence and inflation dynamics in Ghana.

## Background

I hold an MSc in Economics and Economic Policy (Research in Economics) from HSE University and a First Class BA in Economics from Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST).

My research experience includes work on maternal healthcare utilisation, women's empowerment, climate adaptation among small-scale farmers in Northern Ghana, and international trade and food productivity.
