## Research agenda

I am interested in applied economic research at the intersection of **international economics, international finance and health economics**. A central question motivating my emerging agenda is how international financial conditions and exchange-rate movements affect the cost, availability and accessibility of essential medicines in developing economies.

My broader interests include development economics, agricultural and environmental economics, monetary institutions and public policy evaluation.

## Primary research areas

### International economics and trade
- Pharmaceutical imports and import dependence.
- Trade exposure and the transmission of international shocks.
- Trade relationships, supplier concentration and supply-chain resilience.

### International finance and macroeconomics
- Exchange-rate pass-through.
- External financial shocks and domestic economic outcomes.
- Central bank independence and monetary stability.

### Health economics
- Medicine affordability and availability.
- Healthcare utilisation and household welfare.
- Health financing and access to essential medicines.

## Related research areas

I also have experience and continuing interests in development economics, gender and household welfare, agricultural economics, climate adaptation and environmental sustainability.

## Selected projects

### Exchange-rate shocks and pharmaceutical trade
**Proposed research direction.** A comparative study of selected African and Asian economies examining exchange-rate movements, pharmaceutical import dependence, medicine affordability and supply-chain resilience. The final country sample and empirical chapters will be determined by verified data coverage.

### Central bank independence and inflation dynamics in Ghana
**Working paper.** Examines the relationship between central bank independence and inflation dynamics in Ghana over 2002–2023, using separately constructed de jure and de facto indices.

### Insurance Without Delivery: Stage-Specific Constraints in Maternal Healthcare Utilisation
**Research manuscript.** Examines Ghana's LEAP 1000 programme and maternal healthcare utilisation, with attention to the distinction between health-insurance coverage and the use of skilled and facility-based delivery services.

### The Daughter Effect: Gender Preference and Women's Empowerment
**Master's thesis.** Uses a difference-in-differences research design to examine the relationship between child gender and women's empowerment in India.

### Climate adaptation among small-scale farmers in Northern Ghana
**Research consultancy.** Contributed research and analytical work to a project examining access to weather information, climate adaptation, farmer resilience and institutional participation.

### International trade and food productivity
**Undergraduate thesis.** Examined the relationship between international trade and food productivity.

## Research methods

My methodological interests include applied econometrics, panel-data methods, causal inference, difference-in-differences designs and quantitative policy evaluation.

I use Stata, R and Python for data management, statistical analysis and research workflows.
